# waytowalk Brand Guidelines & Rebranding Rollout

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-11-08  
**Status:** Standalone identity package; application integration has not been performed.

---

## 1. Brand foundation

### Name

**waytowalk**

Write the brand name in lowercase in logos, navigation, marketing, email, documents, and social profiles. Do not write “WayToWalk”, “Way to Walk”, “way2walk”, or “WAYTOWALK”. Capitalisation required by a legal entity name is separate from the product brand.

### Tagline

**Real journeys. Your way.**

Use the exact wording and punctuation. The tagline expresses the two sides of the product: trips grounded in real places, routes, travellers, and agencies; and the freedom to copy, adapt, and plan a journey collaboratively.

### Purpose

waytowalk helps travellers discover real trips, turn them into practical day-by-day plans, adapt them to geography and weather, plan with other people, manage trip money, and work with travel agencies. AI removes the blank page and supports decisions; it is not the brand’s hero.

### Positioning statement

**waytowalk is the collaborative travel platform where real trips become your own—planned with people, grounded in place, and supported by AI when it helps.**

### Brand attributes

- **Grounded:** real geography, realistic pacing, practical details, and accountable agency interactions.
- **Human:** warm, collaborative, inclusive, and never defined by automation alone.
- **Open:** discovery, shared plans, adaptable routes, and multiple ways to travel.
- **Clear:** plain language, calm interfaces, and honest constraints.
- **Curious:** forward movement and exploration without clichés or exaggeration.

### Short boilerplate

**Discover real trips, plan together, and make every journey your own with waytowalk.**

### Long boilerplate

**waytowalk brings trip discovery, collaborative planning, maps, weather-aware itineraries, budgets, and travel-agency proposals into one place. Start from a real journey or a blank page, adapt the route with your group, and keep the plan practical from first idea to final export.**

## 2. Logo system

![waytowalk primary logo](assets/waytowalk-logo-primary.svg)

The symbol is a continuous trail forming a **W**. Its bends represent an adaptable itinerary rather than a rigid route. The coral waypoint marks discovery, a shared decision, or an arrival still being shaped. The name uses a lowercase Manrope wordmark to keep the identity approachable and contemporary.

### Supplied assets

|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: symbol, name, tagline | [SVG](assets/waytowalk-logo-primary.svg) | [PNG](assets/waytowalk-logo-primary.png) | Marketing pages, presentations, brand introductions |
| Name: symbol and name | [SVG](assets/waytowalk-logo-name.svg) | [PNG](assets/waytowalk-logo-name.png) | Navigation, email, documents, general product use |
| Reversed: symbol and name | [SVG](assets/waytowalk-logo-reverse.svg) | [PNG](assets/waytowalk-logo-reverse.png) | Forest, dark, or dark photographic backgrounds |
| Full-colour symbol | [SVG](assets/waytowalk-symbol.svg) | [PNG](assets/waytowalk-symbol.png) | Favicon source, app icon, avatar, compact placements |

The SVGs reference the bundled Manrope files in `../fonts/`. Keep the `assets` and `fonts` folders together when moving the package. PNG exports are raster artwork; SVG is preferred whenever the destination supports it.

### Choosing a lockup

1. Use the **primary lockup** when introducing the brand and space allows the tagline to remain legible.
2. Use the **name lockup** for routine product navigation and communication. This is the default logo.
3. Use the **symbol** only where the waytowalk name is already clear from context, such as a favicon or signed-in app header.
4. Use the **reversed logo** on dark surfaces. Do not place the forest logo on forest or similarly dark colours.
5. Use the **single-colour symbol** when production permits only one ink.

### Clear space

Let **x** equal the diameter of the coral waypoint. Keep at least **1× x** of empty space on every side of the logo. For crowded navigation or document headers, **1.5× x** is preferred. Clear space must be free of text, borders, other logos, and important photographic detail.

### Minimum sizes

| Lockup | Digital minimum | Print minimum |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primary with tagline | 240 px wide | 60 mm wide |
| Name without tagline | 140 px wide | 35 mm wide |
| Symbol | 24 px wide | 8 mm wide |

Below these sizes, switch to a simpler lockup rather than scaling the artwork further. For very small favicons, use the symbol and test at 16, 32, and 48 px.

### Colour versions

- **Full colour:** forest symbol and name, coral waypoint, forest tagline.
- **Reversed:** ivory symbol and name, coral waypoint.
- **Single colour:** use forest, black, or white as one complete colour; do not leave the waypoint in a second colour.
- Do not introduce gradients, transparency effects, shadows, outlines, bevels, or multicolour routes.

### Background control

Preferred backgrounds are warm ivory, white, soft sage, and other quiet light neutrals. Use the reversed logo on deep forest or charcoal. Over photography, choose an uncluttered area with stable contrast; otherwise add a solid or sufficiently opaque forest panel behind a reversed logo. Do not place the logo directly over detailed maps, faces, text, or busy scenery.

### Misuse

Do not:

- stretch, crop, rotate, skew, or rearrange the symbol and wordmark;
- typeset a replacement wordmark in another font;
- uppercase or add spaces to the name;
- alter the route geometry or move the waypoint;
- recolour individual elements outside the supplied versions;
- add a container, badge, glow, shadow, gradient, or 3D treatment;
- add AI sparkles, aircraft, globes, compasses, suitcases, or generic map pins;
- reproduce the tagline at sizes where it becomes unreadable.

### Colour balance

Use approximately **60% ivory/white**, **25% forest and deep forest**, **10% sage and neutral surfaces**, and **5% coral**. Coral is a waypoint, not a wash. Avoid large coral backgrounds and reintroducing the former blue/teal gradients.

### Accessibility rules

Verified contrast ratios:

| Foreground / background | Ratio | Approved use |
|---|---:|---|
| Forest on Warm Ivory | 10.54:1 | Body text, buttons, headings |
| White on Forest | 11.67:1 | Primary button and navigation text |
| Muted Forest on Warm Ivory | 5.54:1 | Secondary body text |
| Tagline Forest on Warm Ivory | 5.73:1 | Tagline and secondary copy |
| Forest on Soft Sage | 5.72:1 | Text and controls on sage surfaces |
| Warm Ivory on Deep Forest | 13.30:1 | Reversed text and logos |
| Forest on Trail Coral | 3.98:1 | Large text only; not normal-size body text |
| White on Trail Coral | 2.93:1 | Do not use for text or standard controls |

**Primary buttons:** Forest background with white text.  
**Secondary buttons:** transparent or ivory background, Forest text and border.  
**Dark-mode primary buttons:** Warm Ivory background with Forest text.  
**Coral:** decorative accents and large graphical elements, not small text or standard text-bearing buttons.  
**Focus indicators:** Forest on light surfaces and Warm Ivory on dark surfaces; do not rely on colour alone for state.

## 4. Typography

### Brand wordmark — Manrope SemiBold

The supplied wordmark uses **Manrope 600** with tight tracking. Always use the supplied logo artwork rather than recreating it with live text.

The package includes:

- `fonts/manrope-regular.ttf`
- `fonts/manrope-semibold.ttf`
- `fonts/OFL-Manrope.txt`

Keep the licence with any redistributed font files.

### Product and communication fonts

| Role | Typeface | Weight | Guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display and editorial headings | **Newsreader** | 500–600 | Warm, confident, concise; use italics only for occasional emphasis |
| UI, body, labels, buttons | **Manrope** | 400–600 | Default product typeface |
| Data-dense emphasis | **Manrope** | 600–700 | Use 700 sparingly for metrics and key totals |
| Logo | **Manrope** | 600 | Supplied artwork only |

Both typefaces are already referenced by the current application. Do not add another display or body family during the rebrand.

### Fallback stacks

```css
font-family: "Manrope", ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", sans-serif;
font-family: "Newsreader", ui-serif, Georgia, serif;
```

Email clients may replace web fonts. Use the supplied system stack for email body copy while retaining the logo as an image.

### Recommended type scale

| Style | Desktop size / line height | Mobile guidance | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | 64 / 68 px | 40 / 44 px | Newsreader 600 |
| H1 | 48 / 54 px | 36 / 42 px | Newsreader 600 |
| H2 | 36 / 44 px | 30 / 38 px | Newsreader 600 |
| H3 | 26 / 34 px | 24 / 32 px | Manrope 600 |

Use sentence case rather than all caps for headings and buttons. Reserve uppercase for short, secondary metadata with increased letter spacing.

## 5. Layout, iconography, and imagery

### Layout

- Use generous negative space and clear route-like reading flows.
- Keep cards and surfaces quiet: thin Trail Border rules, limited shadow, and no decorative gradients.
- Retain the existing rounded product geometry; roughly 12 px card and control radii fit the brand.
- Use the trail motif as a subtle connector in illustrations or section dividers, not as a repeated background texture.
- Keep taglines and marketing copy concise; do not crowd the logo with supporting text.

### Iconography

Use the existing **Lucide** icon family with rounded caps and joins. Keep stroke weights consistent, normally 1.75–2 px at UI sizes. Route, map, calendar, group, weather, budget, and document icons should remain functional and familiar. Do not mix filled travel clip art with the interface icon set.

### Photography and illustration

Choose real places, credible travel moments, natural light, and scenes that show scale, terrain, local transit, food, stays, or people making decisions together. Avoid generic luxury-resort imagery, impossible viewpoints, exaggerated saturation, and AI-themed robots or glowing brains. Illustrations may simplify maps and routes using Forest, Sage, and one Coral waypoint.

### Maps

Maps are working tools, not brand decoration. Keep labels legible and controls familiar. Use Forest for the active route, Sage for secondary routes, and Coral for a selected waypoint. Do not use colour alone to distinguish route state; add labels, patterns, or icons where needed.

### Agency and partner co-branding

An agency’s identity leads on its own public profile, package, quote, and proposal. The waytowalk name lockup may appear as a smaller platform attribution such as “Powered by waytowalk”. Do not recolour the waytowalk logo to match an agency. Preserve clear space and avoid combining both logos into a single lockup.

## 6. Voice and messaging

### Voice

- **Plain:** “See the route, weather, and cost before you decide.”
- **Collaborative:** “Plan together” rather than “Let our algorithm take over.”
- **Grounded:** “A practical plan you can edit” rather than “The perfect trip instantly.”
- **Warm:** invite curiosity without excessive exclamation marks or travel clichés.
- **Honest:** do not imply booking, payments, live inventory, or autonomous actions the product does not provide.

### Preferred language

Use **trip**, **journey**, **route**, **plan together**, **discover**, **adapt**, **real places**, and **travel agency**. Use **AI planner** or **assistant** only when describing the relevant capability.

Avoid **revolutionary**, **magical**, **flawless**, **instant dream vacation**, **AI-powered everything**, and unsupported promises about price, availability, safety, or bookings.

### Example homepage copy

**Eyebrow:** REAL TRIPS, READY TO MAKE YOUR OWN  
**Headline:** Find a journey. Walk it your way.  
**Body:** Discover trips from travellers and agencies, adapt the route with your group, and keep the plan, map, weather, and budget together.  
**Primary CTA:** Start planning  
**Secondary CTA:** Explore trips

### Tagline placement

Use “Real journeys. Your way.” in brand introductions, presentations, social graphics, and selected marketing footers. Routine navigation and compact product headers should use the name-only lockup. Do not attach multiple alternative taglines to the same identity.

## 7. Digital and document applications

### Product navigation

Use the name-only SVG at approximately 140–180 px wide, respecting clear space. On small screens, the symbol may replace the full lockup only when an accessible label identifies the link as “waytowalk home”.

### Favicon and app icons

Build favicon and app-icon sizes from `waytowalk-symbol.svg`. Test the silhouette at 16 px. For an app store or social avatar, centre the symbol on Warm Ivory with clear space; for a transparent favicon, omit the background.

### Social sharing

Use the primary lockup on Warm Ivory, with the logo occupying no more than roughly half the canvas width. Keep all artwork inside the central safe area so cropping does not remove the tagline. Do not use a transparent PNG as the final OpenGraph image when the destination’s background is unknown; export a solid ivory social card instead.

### Email

Use the name-only logo in the header, Forest for the primary CTA, and system-font body copy. Update sender display names, footers, invitation copy, password resets, lifecycle reminders, and plain-text alternatives together.

### PDFs and proposals

Use the name-only logo for platform-authored exports. Preserve agency-owned branding on agency documents. Forest is suitable for headings and rules; Coral should be limited to small highlights. Verify that single-colour printing remains legible and that the tagline is omitted when too small.

## 8. Rebranding implementation steps

These are instructions for a later implementation pass. No application changes are included in this package.

### Phase 1 — Approve and prepare

1. Approve the lowercase name, tagline, logo geometry, colours, and voice in this document.
2. Confirm legal naming, domain, social handles, email sender domain, and trademark availability separately; their availability is not assumed by this guide.
3. Keep `brand/assets/` and `brand/fonts/` together as the source package.
4. Create an integration branch and record the current visual baselines for light/dark, desktop/mobile, email, and exported documents.
5. Inventory public-facing uses of `TravelAI`, `Travel AI`, and `@TravelAI`. Treat internal storage keys, deployment identifiers, and historical content as migration decisions rather than performing a blind global replacement.

### Phase 2 — Install the identity assets

1. Copy the required production assets into the application’s public asset directory during implementation; do not reference this source folder at runtime.
2. Replace the navigation wordmark in `frontend/src/components/Navbar.tsx` with the name-only SVG and an accessible “waytowalk home” label.
3. Replace the inline aircraft favicon in `frontend/index.html` with artwork derived from `waytowalk-symbol.svg`.
4. Create favicon, social-avatar, and solid-background OpenGraph exports from the supplied masters.
5. Keep the SVG and font licence available in the source asset inventory.

### Phase 3 — Apply colour and typography

1. Update the global HSL tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` using the palette and accessibility rules above.
2. Replace sky-blue/teal primary treatments and travel gradients with Forest, Ivory, Sage, and restrained Coral accents.
3. Review the separate `.agency-theme` and `.agency-theme.dark` tokens so agency workspaces follow the same platform identity without overriding agency-authored document brands.
4. Update dark-mode surfaces and primary-button colours explicitly; do not merely invert the light palette.
5. Keep the existing Manrope and Newsreader configuration in `frontend/tailwind.config.ts` and `frontend/index.html`.
6. Inspect forms, maps, charts, badges, focus states, disabled states, weather risk colours, and proposal comparison tables for contrast and semantic clarity.

### Phase 4 — Rename the traveller and public experience

1. Update visible names, aria labels, page titles, and descriptions in `frontend/index.html` and `frontend/src/components/Navbar.tsx`.
2. Review traveller, authentication, sharing, gallery, profile, event, proposal, and agency-public pages found under `frontend/src/pages/` and `frontend/src/components/`.
3. Update `frontend/src/pages/Landing.tsx` and `frontend/src/components/LandingChat.tsx` so AI supports the story rather than leading it.
4. Update shared-trip and proposal metadata and visible footers, not only signed-in navigation.
5. Use sentence-case “waytowalk” consistently and apply the messaging rules in this guide.

### Phase 5 — Rename the assistant safely

1. Coordinate `@TravelAI` changes across `frontend/src/components/dashboard/CommentsTab.tsx`, `frontend/src/hooks/useTripComments.ts`, `backend/src/services/collab/assistant.ts`, assistant schemas, routes, and tests.
2. Rename the visible assistant and prompt identity together; do not change only the composer label while the backend still requires the old mention.
3. Decide how historical messages containing `@TravelAI` should display and whether old mentions must remain recognised during a controlled migration.
4. Update assistant tests and run an end-to-end encrypted-chat check after the rename.

### Phase 6 — Update email, exports, and agency surfaces

1. Update the default sender display name in `backend/src/config/env.ts` and configure the production `EMAIL_FROM` value separately.
2. Update HTML and plain-text templates in `backend/src/services/mail/templates.ts`, including invitations, replies, password resets, reminders, and footers.
3. Update personal PDF branding in `frontend/src/lib/pdfExport.ts` and platform-authored export surfaces under `backend/src/services/agency/exports/`.
4. Review built-in templates and seed content. Do not silently rewrite agency-owned custom templates, uploaded artwork, quotes, or historical documents.
5. Check emails and PDFs in light, dark, print, and single-colour contexts where applicable.

### Phase 7 — Handle internal identifiers deliberately

1. `travelai_theme` appears in `frontend/public/theme-init.js` and `frontend/src/components/Navbar.tsx`. Either retain it as a documented internal key or migrate both locations together while preserving each user’s saved theme.
2. Review package metadata in root, frontend, and backend `package.json` files.
3. Review `render.yaml`, deployment names, environment examples, email domains, and public origins. Changing a Render service name can affect deployment and hostnames; plan it separately from the visual release.
4. Do not rename database collections, cryptographic keys, cookie settings, API contracts, or tenant identifiers solely for cosmetic consistency.
5. Update `README.md` and relevant documentation after product behaviour and screenshots reflect the new identity.

### Phase 8 — Verify before launch

1. Search source for remaining `TravelAI`, `Travel AI`, `@TravelAI`, sky-blue brand tokens, and the old aircraft favicon; classify every intentional remainder.
2. Verify all transparent assets on white, ivory, sage, forest, and photographic backgrounds.
3. Test navigation and logo legibility at 320 px mobile width through large desktop sizes, in light and dark themes.
4. Check keyboard focus, reduced motion, text contrast, non-text contrast, and accessible names.
5. Exercise traveller, agency, admin, shared-trip, proposal, and guest-enquiry routes.
6. Send representative emails and render personal and agency PDFs; inspect both screen and print output.
7. Test `@waytowalk` assistant mentions and historical chat behaviour if the mention is renamed.
8. Validate favicon and social-sharing previews on their intended destinations.
9. Run the repository’s full verification command: `npm run verify`.

### Phase 9 — Launch and maintain

1. Deploy the coordinated UI, backend, email, and metadata changes together.
2. Update deployment environment values, public profiles, app listings, support material, and social avatars.
3. Refresh cached social previews where the destination provides a supported refresh mechanism.
4. Monitor navigation, authentication, assistant mentions, email delivery, exports, and public sharing after release.
5. Keep this guide and the supplied masters as the single brand source of truth; record future changes with a new version and date.

## 9. Release acceptance checklist

### Identity

- [ ] Name appears as `waytowalk` everywhere public-facing.
- [ ] Tagline appears exactly as `Real journeys. Your way.` where used.
- [ ] Navigation uses the name-only lockup; marketing introductions may use the primary lockup.
- [ ] Small placements use the symbol without an unreadable tagline.
- [ ] Clear space, minimum size, and background rules are respected.

### Design

- [ ] Forest, Coral, Ivory, and Sage replace the previous blue/teal identity.
- [ ] Manrope and Newsreader are used consistently; no additional family is introduced.
- [ ] Primary buttons meet contrast requirements.
- [ ] Coral is not used for small text or normal-size text-bearing buttons.
- [ ] Dark mode and agency-scoped themes are reviewed separately.

### Product surfaces

- [ ] Metadata, favicon, navigation, authentication, landing, and footer are updated.
- [ ] Shared trips, public profiles, proposals, guest pages, emails, and PDFs are updated.
- [ ] Assistant composer, backend mention gate, prompt identity, and tests agree.
- [ ] Agency-owned content and historical documents are handled deliberately.
- [ ] Internal storage and deployment identifiers are migrated only with their dependencies.

### Assets and quality

- [ ] SVG masters open with the bundled Manrope fonts.
- [ ] PNGs have genuine alpha transparency and no white or ivory matte.
- [ ] Reversed artwork is visible on Deep Forest.
- [ ] The symbol remains recognisable at favicon sizes.
- [ ] `npm run verify` passes and visual QA is complete.
